Guide / Coach

Using SEE as a coach.

Every athlete you're linked to feeds real data into your dashboard — not a survey they fill out once, but what they actually logged after every climb. This guide covers the four things you'll use most: your roster, the two per-athlete insight panels, and logging a climb yourself, live at the wall.

Start here

The roster

Athletes linked to you. Tap any athlete for their assignments, session notes, and chat — this is home base for the whole dashboard.

  1. The top strip is your whole team at a glance: athlete count, total logged sessions, team-wide Learning % and send rate, and how many athletes are currently flagged — a real, threshold-based flag (repeated panic-zone sessions in a short window), not a guess.
  2. Each row shows that athlete's own zone-mix bar and, when it applies, a specific flag like "4 panic sessions this week" — nothing shown for an athlete with nothing to flag.
  3. To link a new athlete, use the Link athlete field at the bottom of the roster with their email — they'll need to already have a SEE account.
  4. Tap an athlete's name to open their detail view, where the Progress and Gap panels below live.

Athlete detail

Progress Over Time

Mental-zone mix and grade progression, by terrain, on the same 12-week rolling window — the same clock as the athlete's own journal. Open any athlete from the roster to see this.

  1. Zone share (%) shows how the Comfort/Learning/Panic mix has shifted week to week — a steady climb in Learning share generally reads as healthy pressure; a rising Panic share on its own is worth a direct conversation.
  2. Grade by terrain plots grade progression separately per terrain type, so you can see technique gains that a single blended grade number would hide — e.g. steady gains on Vertical while Overhang stalls.
  3. This panel is explicitly descriptive, not diagnostic — it's the same underlying logs as the gap panel below, just sliced by calendar week instead of by terrain. They're not meant to produce matching numbers.

Athlete detail

Where's the gap?

Comfort vs. panic, broken down by terrain, across an athlete's full logged history. This is the fastest way to spot a real structural gap — not "they're inconsistent," but specifically where.

  1. The headline names the athlete's strongest and weakest terrain directly — "Most comfortable on Slab... Least comfortable on Overhang" — pulled straight from their real logged sessions, not a self-report.
  2. Below that, each terrain type gets its own comfort/learning/panic bar, so you can see the shape of the gap, not just which terrain is weakest.
  3. If an athlete's only logged one terrain so far, or their comfort and panic zones peak on the same terrain, the panel says so plainly instead of forcing a comparison that isn't real yet.
  4. This panel is all-time and terrain-only — no week-over-week trend here. For trend, use Progress Over Time above.

At the wall

Logging a climb for an athlete

Standing at the wall watching an athlete work a route is exactly when you don't want to hand them a phone. From an athlete's detail page, tap Log a climb to open a fast, coach-side entry.

  1. The zone is the only required field — Comfort, Learning, or Panic, one tap. Everything else (which climb, terrain, grade, a short note) is optional and can be skipped entirely.
  2. If you've built out a gym climb catalog (wall/lane, angle, terrain), you can pick the exact route and it fills in the terrain and name for you.
  3. After saving, a Log another option keeps the same athlete and climb selection and only resets the zone — built for watching someone work the same route through several attempts back to back.
  4. Anything you log this way is visibly tagged Logged by coach everywhere it shows up — in the athlete's own journal and Home feed too — so it's never mistaken for their own self-log.

Programming

Assigning a workout

The New assignment button next to any athlete on the roster (see the roster screenshot above) opens the assign wizard — search your workout library or an athlete's own custom workouts, set a due date, and it lands directly in their Home screen's "Due this week" list.

  1. You can assign something from the shared workout library, one of your own custom-built workouts, or write a fully freeform assignment with no linked workout at all.
  2. Assignments with a linked workout get a real Start session button on the athlete's end, which opens the actual session builder pre-filled — not just a checkbox.
  3. Marking an assignment complete is a separate action from the athlete logging reps — completing the assignment and actually doing the work are tracked independently, on purpose.
